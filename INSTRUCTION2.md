The Mistbound Caravan Game Expansion Plan

English design revision for one GM and eight players

Version 3 planning baseline • 15 October 2026

Build a local fantasy adventure in which eight friends explore, negotiate, solve problems, and coordinate while one GM runs the world. Some journeys are fully cooperative. Selected journeys add hidden agendas and bounded sabotage. The laptop hosts the application, the TV shows shared events, and phones hold individual choices and information.

The central change is variety of decisions. A new mission should change what players reason about and how they interact, rather than merely replace the bridge question or assign a different traitor. Acting remains optional. No player leaves the session because of a vote, an accusation, or a failed challenge.

This plan refines the supplied GM and secret agenda proposal. It replaces that proposal's instant victory for a wrongly accused traitor, unrestricted clue falsification, automatic personality labels, and four table database shortcut with explicit playable contracts.

## Relationship to the earlier design

The earlier English game design remains the reference for the original caravan setting and baseline mission. For this GM expansion, the rules in this plan take precedence wherever they differ: one GM plus eight players, two squads of four, selectable relationship mode, revised Alarm behavior, bounded intrigue, and separate mission and agenda outcomes. Do not combine old and new threshold rules in the same session.

## How to use this plan

Sections 1–5 define the group and variety system. Sections 6–8 specify intrigue. Sections 9–10 provide a complete first mission baseline. Sections 11–14 expand the season, NPCs, and procedural content. Sections 15–18 cover implementation and validation. Section 19 is a development agent brief; section 20 maps the supplied references to design choices.

All mechanics and mission examples below are design proposals. Numerical settings require playtesting. The plan does not represent an implemented application or a proven balance model.

# 1 Group configuration and game modes

The main table has nine people total. Thái Bảo is the GM and does not occupy a player seat, receive a secret agenda, submit player actions, or vote. The eight named profiles are convenience defaults; names, seats, squads, and public roles remain editable before a mission.

Use two squads of four for the first eight player release. Squad membership organizes tasks, not allegiance. Do not make one squad secretly hostile to the other. Three squads of three, three, and two remain a later cooperative configuration requiring its own challenge validation.

## Select the relationship mode before assigning roles

**Cooperative Journey:** everyone pursues the caravan's mission. Optional personal goals recognize fictional achievements without sabotaging the group. This is the recommended tutorial and first complete session.

**Intrigue Journey:** exactly six Loyal Crew, one Saboteur, and one Independent Broker. Show these counts to everyone. Randomize agendas server side, separately from names, squads, and character roles. Only packs explicitly tested for intrigue may enable it. Do not add an unannounced second saboteur.

Intrigue is initially supported for exactly eight active player seats. Cooperative support for four through eight players is a later scaling milestone. A missing phone does not change the active roster or secretly remove an agenda. Pause and reconnect; if someone must leave permanently, save the mission or end it as interrupted rather than letting the GM play their hidden role.

Use an optional anonymous lobby choice between cooperation and intrigue. Start cooperation if anyone requests it; publish only the selected mode. Do not reveal which person preferred which mode.

# 2 Shared rules and resolution baseline

The same public rules operate in both modes. Secret agendas modify only explicitly declared windows and the final security check. The GM cannot silently change costs or thresholds after seeing submissions.

| **Meter** | **Initial value and bounds** | **Meaning**                         |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Supplies  | 6 within 0–12                | Shared purchases and repairs        |
| Integrity | 5 within 0–6                 | Vehicle durability                  |
| Alarm     | 0 within 0–6                 | Journey pressure and ending quality |

**Alarm revision:** six Alarm after a complete resolution event marks the journey urgent; it does not immediately skip scenes. Once marked, urgency remains even if Alarm later falls. This preserves the finale. Integrity zero takes priority and enters a short evacuation ending. All eight players still make its final salvage choice.

## Public roles and effort

Each player chooses Scout, Engineer, or Guardian and receives one role use per mission. Scout opens the first hint of one scene without its usual one Alarm cost. Engineer discounts one repair or purchased shield by one Supply, minimum price one; discounts never stack. Guardian cancels one Integrity loss in their own squad during the response window. Only one Guardian may cancel a particular loss.

Everyone also receives one Effort. Before a shared risk roll, accept up to four committed Effort tokens, each adding one to a server d20. If more are offered, select four by rotating seat priority; retain unselected tokens. Success is total at least DC 10, 13, or 16 as stated by the option. There is no automatic natural one or twenty rule. At DC 13, success is 40 percent with no Effort and 60 percent with four. Save the roll; reconnecting does not reroll it.

## Common input rules

Selections can change until closure. Resolve when all eight are Ready and the GM confirms, or at the server deadline. Default a missing shared vote to abstain, a private arrangement to Skip, and a storm action to Shield in that player's squad. Ready itself does not apply effects.

Ordinary shared choices use plurality. A tied choice goes to the next seat in a rotating priority order for twenty seconds, then a published option priority. All abstaining uses the stated default. Watch and final accusation use their separate five vote threshold. Queue concurrent changes and apply each accepted resolution once.

# 3 The GM as facilitator and world operator

The GM knows authored answers and hidden agendas. That knowledge makes them a facilitator rather than a competing player. Their job is to describe situations, portray NPCs if desired, help with rules, and keep consequences consistent.

## Useful powers

- Pause, resume, extend a timer, or wait for a disconnected phone.
- Reveal an earned clue, explain a published rule, or repeat instructions.
- Select a prepared branch that follows the group's decision.
- Accept an alternative approach using the scene's declared equivalence rules.
- View the private event record and resolve a technical interruption.

The GM must not invent an extra saboteur, falsify authenticated evidence, give a preferred player secret resources, reveal a hidden agenda through narration, or change the committed result. An unexpected player idea can be resolved without pretending the engine understands unrestricted natural language.

## Alternative approaches

Each scene declares whether it supports bargain, investigate, repair, distract, or bypass. When a player suggests something else, the GM maps it to an existing approach, states the cost and outcome range publicly, and receives group acceptance before commitment. A genuinely new effect requires an explicit logged ruling. Such a ruling cannot change an agenda or identify the Saboteur.

## Bounded surprises

An authored event deck may supply at most two optional complications per mission, each lasting at most one scene and imposing at most one Supply loss or one Alarm gain. Draw only at declared checkpoints. Use them in cooperative mode first; leave them disabled during the initial intrigue tests so environmental events do not bury evidence of sabotage.

## GM information surfaces

Keep a private timeline of action, actor, resource delta, cause, and visibility. The TV displays only the authorized public projection. When the GM opens the full timeline, it must never appear in the TV window or a player payload. A separate public narration preview helps prevent accidental reading of private logs aloud.

Allow plain speech and phone choices in every scene. NPC performance, accents, deception speeches, and elaborate backstories are optional. The game should produce discussion through conflicting needs and useful information, without requiring confident acting.

# 4 Ten challenge families

Implement three families first and expand gradually. Each family changes a different part of the decision, so the season does not become six versions of the same allocation puzzle.

| **Family**              | **Core interaction**                        | **Example**                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Distributed deduction   | Combine private facts                       | Determine which bridge reaches the shelter      |
| Simultaneous allocation | Divide limited actions                      | Protect lamps while powering the vehicle        |
| Negotiation             | Exchange offers under constraints           | Buy medicine without surrendering a needed part |
| Investigation           | Choose leads and compare evidence           | Reconstruct who moved the missing cargo         |
| Route exploration       | Spend time to reveal alternatives           | Explore a marsh shortcut or repair a causeway   |
| Push your luck          | Decide whether another reward is worth risk | Salvage one more compartment before fog arrives |
| Assembly and planning   | Submit complementary components             | Align a rune circuit with shared power limits   |
| Creature interaction    | Infer a goal and change the environment     | Redirect a bell collector without fighting      |
| Auction and valuation   | Compare private values and limited bids     | Allocate expedition equipment between factions  |
| Forecast and expression | Predict fictional preferences or patterns   | Guess which offer a peculiar market will favor  |

The first engine release contains deduction, allocation, and constrained negotiation. Investigation follows once clue visibility and scene transitions work reliably. Later families reuse common input and resolution services; they are not mandatory launch features.

## What makes two scenes meaningfully different

Change the information structure, objective, available actions, cost of failure, or relationship between players. Recoloring a bridge changes presentation. Requiring two squads to build compatible halves of a mechanism changes interaction. Adding a clock changes pressure but does not, by itself, create a new family.

Every major scene gives each player a clue, an owned offer, a configuration part, or an action. Avoid eight people waiting for one spokesperson to finish a puzzle. Individual contributions can be brief and simultaneous.

Use a one minute practice example before an unfamiliar family. Optional drawing, sound, or dexterity variants must offer an equivalent phone selection route. Do not make microphone access or fast reactions a requirement for the main story.

# 5 Mission recipes and changing pace

A mission combines a small number of families around one assignment. Target sixty to ninety minutes including discussion and the ending. These are initial pacing targets, not measured completion times.

| **Recipe**         | **Main sequence**                                 | **Emotional rhythm**                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Rescue journey     | Route choice, deduction, allocation               | Discover, coordinate, relieve pressure     |
| Market intrigue    | Negotiation, investigation, custody decision      | Temptation, suspicion, commitment          |
| Forest expedition  | Exploration, creature interaction, push your luck | Curiosity, uncertainty, retreat or risk    |
| Mechanism recovery | Investigation, assembly, allocation               | Understanding, planning, execution         |
| Depot festival     | Forecast, auction, short assembly                 | Light competition, surprise, cooperation   |
| Season finale      | Negotiation, branching callback, allocation       | Recognition, difficult choice, consequence |

Use approximately three principal families per mission, plus short decisions. Avoid repeating the same dominant family in consecutive missions. Usually place only one long deduction scene in a session. Add one unfamiliar mechanic at a time and explain how it differs from familiar controls.

## A practical time budget

Allow eight minutes for joining and practice, eight for the opening decision, twelve for each of two major scenes, five for a rest, fifteen for the climax, and eight for the ending and recap. This totals about sixty eight minutes before extra discussion. Extend or shorten the conversation, rather than silently accelerating reading timers.

An intrigue overlay uses the rest and climax windows already in the mission. Reserve an additional five to ten minutes for evidence and final discussion. Do not append a full separate Werewolf game after an already complete adventure.

## A choice should change a later scene

Rescuing passengers increases a later protection need. Trading a bell solves a creature encounter but removes a market bargaining item. Opening a shortcut changes who can deliver supplies in the finale. Each choice has an immediate understandable cost and a visible later callback.

The author maintains a small family history for the crew. Recommend underused families for the next mission, then let the GM choose. Never change the live rules because telemetry reports that the group is winning quickly.

# 6 Secret agendas with bounded stakes

In intrigue mode, public character role and private agenda are independent. A Guardian can be the Saboteur. The agenda screen explains its own actions, all factions' win conditions, and the fact that nobody is eliminated.

| **Agenda**         | **Count** | **Personal success condition**                                       |
| ------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Loyal Crew         | 6         | The wick is delivered and the guild seal is secured                  |
| Saboteur           | 1         | Delivery occurs, at least one Plot succeeded, and the seal is stolen |
| Independent Broker | 1         | Delivery occurs and two personal Coins were earned                   |

Delivery may be normal or urgent. Evacuation means these agenda conditions fail; personal Coins and Plots remain in the recap but do not create a win. The Broker stays until the ending and continues taking normal actions. They do not leave the table after earning Coins.

## Separate the mission and the contest

Mission outcome is Delivered, Urgent Delivery, or Evacuated. Security outcome is Secured or Stolen, with Not Applicable on evacuation. Agenda success is calculated separately for each player. A stolen seal creates a guild relationship complication; it does not erase the rescued family or the delivered wick.

The Saboteur targets an optional guild seal, not the essential lantern wick. This lets the betrayal matter without retroactively invalidating the whole adventure. A failed accusation alone never steals anything.

## Limits on interference

The Saboteur starts with two Intrigue tokens, usable only in the two declared windows in section 7. Each accepted Plot costs one and has a maximum public impact of one Supply lost or one Alarm gained. There is no sabotage action that directly removes Integrity, changes a solved answer, steals another player's phone input, or skips a scene.

Any player can choose an ordinary personal deal. To earn one Coin, spend either their unused Effort or their unused public role ability. The spent resource becomes unavailable for the rest of the mission. Each window allows at most one deal per player. Two Coins require both resources across the two windows, creating a clear cost to the Broker's goal.

Do not assign agendas from past behavior or lock a real person into a villain role. Randomize again for every mission. Keep tutorial practice free of betrayal and reveal all agendas together only after resolution.

# 7 Private windows and evidence integrity

Private arrangements are a short additional selection, separate from a player's ordinary scene action. They never replace a storm Shield, Supply, or Repel submission. All phone screens use the same neutral heading and comparable visual layout.

| **Window**        | **Timing**                             | **Permitted Plot and public effect**     |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| W1 cargo stop     | After rest repair, before Watch voting | Spend one Intrigue and remove one Supply |
| W2 storm interval | After round two normal effects         | Spend one Intrigue and add one Alarm     |

At either window, choose Skip, a personal deal, or the agenda's permitted Plot. A player cannot deal and Plot in the same window. W1 Plot is available only if Supplies are at least one; W2 Plot only if Alarm is below six after ordinary resolution. An unavailable action consumes nothing and defaults to Skip with a private explanation. A Plot counts only when its public resource effect actually occurs.

Commit all choices before revealing any of them. Apply accepted deals and the single possible Plot once. Clamp the resource, update the urgency flag, store the outcome, then publish a neutral summary. Reveal shared resource changes and the number of deals, but not their owners, agenda, squad, individual action timing, or individual spending balances.

## Truth and claims are different objects

**Authenticated evidence** is generated from canonical game facts. Its text, numbers, issuer, and history are immutable. A Share action publishes exactly that evidence. Neither player nor GM may edit a system clue into a false one.

**Player claims** may be true, mistaken, or deliberately misleading. A player can write a short claim or select a suggested statement. The TV labels it as an unverified player claim and shows its author. It must not imitate an authenticated card. Disable unrestricted text if moderation would distract the group.

## Investigating sabotage

After each window that actually contains a Plot, reveal a trace that narrows access to four possible seats, including the actor. The pack generates and saves that access group from the committed event. It explicitly says access is not proof. The second trace, if present, must leave at least two candidates after intersection; avoid turning two automated clues into a certain identity reveal.

Trace cards are never invented for Skip or a deal. If no Plot occurs, report no confirmed tampering. World mystery clues remain independently solvable even when players make false claims.

# 8 Watch voting and the final security decision

After W1, allow one optional Watch vote. Everyone selects one seat or abstains. Exactly one seat can be selected, requiring at least five of eight votes. A split vote, tie, or insufficient majority produces no Watch. If a seat qualifies, ask the group to confirm the stated one Supply cost using the ordinary shared choice rule; if unaffordable or declined, apply nothing.

A watched player retains their public role, Effort, speech, normal actions, and final vote. Only their W2 private arrangement is blocked and defaults to Skip. The restriction expires after W2. It does not reveal their agenda or automatically stop the final Theft. A wrong Watch therefore has a limited, visible cost without removing a friend from play.

## Final sequence after the storm

1 Discuss the evidence for ninety seconds, extendable by the GM.

2 Vote whether to secure the seal chest for two Supplies. Default is do not purchase. Pay once on a valid approved purchase; unaffordable protection cannot be selected.

3 Submit the final accusation privately: one seat or abstain. A seat is identified only with at least five votes. There is at most one identified seat.

4 At the same commitment deadline, the Saboteur privately chooses Attempt Theft or Stay Hidden. Missing input means Stay Hidden. Other agendas receive a neutral Finish selection; they cannot invoke Theft.

5 Freeze inputs and calculate delivery, security, and all agenda results before revealing identities.

The seal is Stolen only when all conditions hold: delivery occurs, the Saboteur has at least one successful Plot, they chose Attempt Theft, the chest was not protected, and they were not the identified seat. Otherwise it is Secured. Identification of a loyal player has no additional effect beyond failing to identify the actual Saboteur.

## Emergency ending

Integrity zero ends ordinary challenges, disables unplayed private windows, and enters a short public salvage decision. Choose which fictional asset rescuers recover; this affects the journal, not retroactive delivery. Security becomes Not Applicable and all delivery dependent agendas fail. Reveal agendas after the salvage result.

Finish with the mission, security, and personal goals on separate lines. A group can deliver the wick while losing the seal; the recap must show both facts. No GM interpretation overrides this outcome table.

# 9 First mission opening and bridge

The Last Lantern Delivery begins with Supplies six, Integrity five, Alarm zero, and urgency false. Use eight players in two squads of four. Deliver a replacement wick to North Lantern Station. A guild seal travels in a separate locked pouch. It has no required role in the bridge solution or wick delivery.

## Ferry decision

**Take the family aboard:** spend one Supply, mark family safe, increase vehicle weight from six to seven, and require one extra Shield in Mechanics during storm round two. Explain the protection consequence immediately.

**Send rescue equipment:** spend two Supplies and mark family safe by boat. No extra weight or storm protection. **Signal another crew:** add one Alarm and mark rescue pending. All three options continue to the bridge.

## Eight private fact cards

1 Vehicle weighs six; the essential cargo is already included and cannot be discarded. 2 Family aboard adds one. 3 North supports eight and leads through Sun. 4 Middle supports ten and leads through Moon. 5 South supports seven and leads through Leaf. 6 Rain is falling upstream. 7 Rain retracts Middle. 8 The destination is beyond Sun.

The unique answer is North for either possible weight. South can support the load but reaches the wrong destination; Middle is unusable and also reaches the wrong gate. Assign one card per player. Every card can be shared authentically. Claims cannot overwrite these facts.

Allow two incorrect group submissions, each adding one Alarm, then explain the answer and continue. Optional staged hints cost one Alarm each, except one Scout first hint. Hints direct attention to destination, gate, and then North. A correct answer needs no die roll.

Cross by repairing for two Supplies, detouring for two Alarm, or risking DC thirteen. A failed risk loses two Integrity; success loses none. Continue unless Integrity reaches zero. Alarm six sets urgency and still continues.

## Short negotiation and rest

A market ferryman offers one storm shield voucher in exchange for the crew's decorative bell. Accepting consumes the bell and makes the first shield purchase cost one Supply instead of two; it does not stack with Engineer. Declining preserves the bell as a memento. Rest then offers one repair: three Supplies for one Integrity, or two with an Engineer. After repair, resolve W1 and the optional Watch in intrigue mode. Cooperative mode skips both.

# 10 First mission storm and endings

The storm has three rounds. Round one threatens Pathfinder; round two threatens Mechanics; round three threatens both squads. Each player chooses Shield in their own squad, Supply, or Repel. A default missing submission is Shield.

| **Requirement**            | **Eight player value**                      | **Result**                              |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Protect a threatened squad | Two Shield contributions                    | Prevent that squad's one Integrity loss |
| Family protection          | Three Shield in Mechanics in round two only | Required when the family is aboard      |
| Power the vehicle          | Three Supply actions anywhere               | Gain one Supply and one power round     |
| Drive back the mist        | Three Repel actions anywhere                | Remove one Alarm                        |

Before each round, the group may buy at most one Shield contribution per squad for two Supplies each. It lasts that round, applies only to protection, and can receive one valid discount. These purchases are public and happen before private normal actions close.

## Apply effects in this order

Freeze actions and compute each threatened squad's loss. Give Guardians ten seconds to cancel losses in their own squad; rotate priority for competing responses to the same loss. Apply remaining Integrity loss and clamp. If Integrity reaches zero, evacuate immediately and do not apply later round effects.

Otherwise award Supply and the power round if its threshold is met, add one Alarm if any Integrity loss remained, apply Repel, clamp each change, and check urgency at the end of these ordinary round effects. After round two, resolve W2 separately and check urgency again. Once urgency is true it remains true.

After round three, fewer than two power rounds adds two Alarm and marks late delivery. Then check urgency. With positive Integrity, deliver the wick normally if urgency is false, or via station rescuers if urgency is true. Record late delivery separately without another penalty. Intrigue then uses section 8's security sequence.

## Feasible coordination example

With the family aboard, round one can use two Pathfinder Shield, three Supply, and three Repel. Round two can use three Mechanics Shield, three Supply, and two other actions. Round three can use two Shield in each squad and four remaining actions. This achieves two power rounds without buying shields, provided players actually cooperate. It is a feasibility example, not proof of balance against betrayal.

Record family safe or pending, station delivery quality, and seal status. Evacuation preserves earlier rescues. The next session resets mission resources rather than inheriting a potentially unwinnable empty vehicle.

# 11 Three missions with different central problems

These are scenario briefs for future packs, not implemented missions. Each must add exact scene data and complete validation before activation.

## The Silent Market

**Families:** negotiation, investigation, auction. A merchant will release medicine only if the crew identifies the rightful owner of a disputed bell. One squad holds delivery records; the other hears three witnesses. The crew can investigate any two leads before trading, or pay extra to avoid delay.

The conclusion is that the bell belongs to the ferry cooperative. Three routes support it: a matching shipment mark, the maker's ledger, and a witness who describes an unrepaired crack. Each route is available independently. Do not require a successful die roll to receive the only necessary clue.

At auction, each player allocates two temporary bid tokens between medicine and repair parts. Bid totals determine which lot the crew receives first; a posted tie order handles equality. Unspent tokens expire. The crew chooses whether to return the bell or negotiate a documented loan. Consequence: ferry trust or an equipment advantage. Intrigue is optional only after a separate certification pass.

## The Switched Signs

**Families:** investigation, route exploration, assembly. Road signs have rotated, but three landmarks stayed fixed. Players each hold one observation or map fragment. Explore the flooded mill, ridge, and watch post in any order; each visit adds one local Travel tick. At three ticks, the shortest route closes, but a longer route remains available.

The solution uses river direction and landmark order rather than compass labels. Players assemble two compatible route halves. Failure costs one extra Travel tick and reveals a useful constraint; it never wipes known clues. Consequence: open a permanent marsh shortcut or establish a safer ridge route. Keep this mission cooperative so players can trust canonical maps while learning exploration.

## Bells in the Forest

**Families:** creature interaction, negotiation, push your luck. A mist creature blocks the road to collect sounds for its frightened young. Clues include its retreat from silence, its protection of a hollow tree, and its choice of bells over food.

Offer a noisy decoy, lend the decorative bell if retained, or take a quiet bypass. The creature retreats after its need is satisfied; it does not fight until its hit points disappear. Optional salvage offers another reward at a published escalating risk. Consequence: a forest ally, a borrowed bell promise, or a cleared bypass. The environment supplies uncertainty without requiring a human traitor.

# 12 Three more missions and a season arc

Variety also comes from why players care about a decision. Alternate rescue, discovery, bargaining, creation, and consequence rather than simply increasing damage numbers.

## The Missing Rune Wheel

**Families:** investigation, assembly, allocation. Retrieve a damaged wheel from an abandoned workshop. Players have complementary component cards; each must place a part or select a diagnostic test. A valid circuit needs six power, no adjacent leaking runes, and a grounded output. Generate clues from the same canonical circuit used by the validator.

The climax divides workers between stabilizing the frame and extracting the wheel. Failed extraction creates a damaged but usable wheel and a repair obligation. A fixed solution pool can provide different circuits, but changing colors alone does not renew a known solution. Consequence: an alternative vehicle configuration or a guild debt.

## The Lantern Depot Festival

**Families:** forecast, auction, short assembly. Give the group a low pressure journey between mysteries. Predict fictional NPC preferences, bid temporary festival tickets for decorative equipment, and assemble a display together. Incorrect predictions produce amusing authored reactions, not a judgment about a real friend's personality.

Use cooperative mode. Rewards are mementos or future narrative alternatives, not permanent bonuses that make festival attendance mandatory. Offer a non drawing choice for any creative activity. Consequence: the crew names its caravan and chooses a public depot tradition.

## The Night at Lantern Depot

**Families:** negotiation, callback decisions, allocation. A mist surge threatens the station, workshop, and ferry. Use three relevant season flags: rescued family or pending rescue, ferry trust, and vehicle condition. Each changes one available offer or rescue route.

The crew chooses which site to stabilize first, then allocates effort to the remaining two. Formerly helped NPCs provide explicit alternative actions. A seal stolen in an earlier intrigue mission causes a guild verification scene, not a permanent resource punishment. Consequence: define the next season's destination and the community the crew has built.

## Player led continuity

At each ending, the crew chooses one of three future leads and may propose another. The GM prepares the selected direction between sessions. Connect personal goals to common places and factions so eight players do not split into eight unrelated campaigns. Read roughly three important flags and write at most three new ones per mission as an authoring guideline, not a hard engine restriction.

# 13 NPCs creatures and meaningful alternatives

An NPC needs a goal, an offer, a boundary, limited knowledge, and a reaction to the crew's choice. This is enough to improvise consistently without preparing pages of dialogue.

| **NPC**                 | **Goal and boundary**                                    | **Offer and consequence**                                   |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ilya the ferryman       | Keep the crossing running; cannot abandon passengers     | Shield voucher for a bell; later transport if trust remains |
| Nara the keeper         | Preserve medicine for residents; refuses false manifests | A documented loan if the crew verifies ownership            |
| Orin the mechanic       | Recover a dangerous wheel; will not endanger apprentices | A repair discount for a safe recovery plan                  |
| Bell gathering creature | Reassure its young; defends the hollow tree              | A clear route for a sound decoy or a promised bell          |

## Three approaches to the same obstacle

A blocked checkpoint can permit **evidence**, using a verified manifest; **bargain**, spending a token or accepting an obligation; or **bypass**, adding time and exposure. Each option needs an explicit cost and continuation. Avoid making one obvious button both cheapest and best in every respect.

NPCs may be mistaken about the world. Label their testimony as testimony and provide a way to check an important claim. An authenticated system card can accurately record that Nara said something without guaranteeing that Nara's statement is true. Evidence types must make this distinction visible.

## Creatures act for reasons

Define the creature's need, what it notices, its preferred tactic, its retreat condition, and a non combat approach. A bridge crawler might protect eggs, cut light sources, and retreat when the crew redirects the lanterns. An intelligent rival might bargain before risking a destructive confrontation.

Use behavioral ideas from the tactics references without importing older monster statistics into the custom allocation engine. If tactical combat is added later, it needs its own turn structure, balance model, and mission scope.

## Improvisation without hidden rule changes

The GM listens, acknowledges a proposed approach, and connects it to a declared consequence. Record accepted promises as explicit flags. A funny performance does not earn extra mechanical power unless the same route is available through a plain spoken or phone selected response.

Recurring people remember what happened. A saved family can become a ferry contact; a guild can request restitution after a missing seal. Keep consequences specific to the fiction rather than inferring which real friends are trustworthy.

# 14 Procedural variation with stable answers

Procedural generation prepares valid scenarios before play. It does not invent new rules while players wait. Use seeded templates, bounded parameter ranges, canonical facts, and an authored fallback.

## Generate in this order

Choose a mission template and seed. Select compatible locations, goals, obstacles, and rewards. Construct canonical state and the intended solution. Derive clues, offers, and valid paths from that state. Validate the complete graph, then save the variant and review it before installation.

| **Variation layer** | **Allowed change**                  | **Required check**                               |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Presentation        | Weather, names, local objects       | No contradiction with established history        |
| Structure           | Lead order, route links, NPC offers | Reachable required scenes and endings            |
| Mechanical          | Capacity, price, task demand        | Feasibility under supported roster and resources |
| Information         | Who knows each fact                 | Necessary facts delivered and answer uniqueness  |
| Intrigue            | Agenda assignment and trace groups  | No identity leakage or false evidence            |

## Two safe templates

**Bridge template:** vary capacities, destination symbols, and weather closure. Enumerate every bridge and prove exactly one satisfies load, availability, and destination for each supported passenger state. Generate all phone cards and the answer key from the resulting facts.

**Negotiation template:** choose three offers with different resource and relationship costs. Check that the minimum required mission item remains obtainable from the initial state along at least one valid path. If all offers require the same missing item, reject the variant.

Limit generation attempts. On exhaustion, use an approved fallback or report an authoring error before the room starts. Never keep a live room waiting indefinitely for an interesting random result.

## Preserve world memory

NPC identities, promises, and established facts persist. Save template version, seed, selected parameters, clue allocation, agenda assignment, and generated trace groups. A reload restores the same world and secret information.

Different names do not make a solved mystery fresh. Replay should change constraints, available approaches, relationships, or authored content. Keep a small solution pool for the first release, then add new validated templates. Optional AI drafting belongs before review and packaging; installed gameplay has no AI API dependency.

Separate the engine from mission data. A mission should add scenes, branches, NPCs, clues, and endings without changing application code when it uses existing families.

| NPC            | id, goal, boundary, knowledge, offers, reactions                 |
| Outcome        | missionResult, securityResult, agendaResults, seasonFlags        |

Use a typed, allowlisted condition and effect language. Examples include resource comparison, flag check, spend resource, set flag, reveal evidence, and transition. Do not accept arbitrary JavaScript, SQL, remote assets, or model generated instructions as executable pack rules.

## Import checks

Validate unique identifiers, all references, asset paths, mode compatibility, numeric bounds, affordability, bounded retries, reachable endings, and the absence of uncontrolled loops. Test that intrigue introduces no condition that makes the public mission structurally impossible. A structurally feasible path does not guarantee victory against deliberate opposition.

A pack cannot enable intrigue merely by setting a boolean. It must declare private windows, trace behavior, security resolution, and supported roster. The first intrigue release validates exactly eight players; other counts must fail import or mode selection clearly.

## Version and restore

Install atomically only after validation. Saves pin pack id, version, digest, and generated variant. Retain old versions needed by saves. Updating a pack does not rewrite a running mission. Upgrade only through an explicit tested migration. Missing versions produce guidance rather than guessed scene mappings.

Bundle local fonts, sound, images, and rules. Prevent path traversal and limit archive size and file types. Commercial books remain author references. Use original game text and assets or material permitted for redistribution; source attribution alone does not authorize copying a complete book.

# 16 Data model and useful session history

Four tables of cumulative counters cannot safely represent rooms, acknowledged inputs, secret visibility, repeated scenes, transactional outcomes, and versioned packs. Keep operational truth separate from derived statistics.

| **Entity**       | **Principal fields and constraints**                                      |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Member           | memberId, displayName, avatar; stable identity, editable name             |
| Session          | sessionId, gmMemberId, mode, rulesVersion, packVersion, seed, status      |
| Participant      | sessionId, memberId, seat, squad, publicRole; unique seat per session     |
| Private state    | participantId, agenda, resources, private evidence; restricted projection |
| Scene instance   | instanceId, sceneId, phase, roundId, deadline, revision, state            |
| Accepted command | commandId, participantId, instanceId, payloadHash, acceptedAt, result     |

| Outcome          | sessionId, missionResult, securityResult, agendaResults, seasonFlags      |
| Snapshot         | revision, state, pausedRemainingTime, pinned content references           |

Store the roster as profiles and assign GM permission per session. Do not make a member's name their credential or permanently attach a secret role to a profile. Player seat ownership requires a reconnect token or explicit GM reassignment.

## Events drive statistics

An accepted input and an applied effect are different events. Record both when necessary, but count only applied actions in awards. Enforce one resolution per scene instance and round, and one effect application per resolution. A command retry returns the stored response, not another purchase or Plot.

Build session summaries from committed events: useful clues shared, repairs applied, rescues chosen, Plots completed, Coins earned, and valid votes. Cache aggregates only with a rebuild path. Do not increment lifetime counters every time an event handler or reconnect runs.

## Feedback that helps content design

Measure time in each phase, hint use, retries, accepted versus missing inputs, and available versus chosen approaches. Exclude pauses and disconnect intervals from decision timing. One slow answer does not establish confusion, disengagement, or a personality trait.

Optional awards should describe a fictional contribution, such as Lamp Keeper or Bridge Reader. Show them after all secrets are revealed. Omit automatic labels such as selfish, slow thinker, or habitual liar. Never use a persistent traitor history to bias the next secret assignment or gate the main story.

## Reconnection and recovery

Persist acknowledged selections and role spending before confirming them. Store the phase, server deadline or paused time, rolls, agenda assignment, generated evidence, and pack version. Restart an unresolved phase paused. Never restore by randomizing roles again.

After ten seconds of a detected disconnect in a timed phase, pause once and let the GM wait or continue using the published defaults. A phone reconnects to its existing seat. When replacing a lost token, GM reassignment invalidates the old device. Names alone cannot reclaim seats.

Resolve commands through one ordered room queue. Use commandId for idempotency and a unique sceneInstanceId plus roundId plus event stage for effects. W2, the ordinary storm round, and the final security check are separate stages. Commit their resulting state and events transactionally before broadcasting results.

## Transport and operation

Use the existing suitable backend if a repository already exists; otherwise the earlier TypeScript and SQLite direction remains a candidate. Bundle assets and keep the database on the laptop. No paid hosting or cloud account is needed for the intended installed LAN session.

Test joining, locking, reconnecting, and resuming on the actual phones. Do not infer nine device compatibility from a build or desktop preview. Provide simple launch instructions, a LAN address and QR code, and an application backup function that includes required pack versions.

## Required rule cases

Check correct and wrong accusations, all abstaining, four versus five votes, insufficient Supplies, a protected chest, no successful Plot, watched Saboteur, watched Broker, unused versus spent abilities, and missing private input. A wrong accusation must never create a Theft by itself.

Check Alarm reaching six during a Plot and remaining urgent after Repel lowers the meter. Check Integrity zero before W2 and after a storm loss; unused windows and Theft remain disabled. Check eight normal storm actions still exist when private deals and Plots are submitted.

Check the bridge at weights six and seven. Check all displayed consequences match actual effects, and every role ability is consumed only when accepted. Crash after acknowledging a deal, during round resolution, and before the final reveal; recovery must preserve choices without duplicated spending or changed secrets.

## Observe enjoyment without inventing scores

After a full session, ask what was interesting, where someone waited, what felt unclear or unfair, and which family they want next. Compare the two modes only after the group has tried each. Use those answers to choose the next pack; do not automatically punish successful play with another traitor or fewer starting resources.

Record agreed changes in the rules version, pack data, and acceptance cases together. If routinely saving two Supplies makes Theft irrelevant, revisit its resource tradeoff before adding more saboteurs. Technical tests establish behavior; group play establishes enjoyment.
